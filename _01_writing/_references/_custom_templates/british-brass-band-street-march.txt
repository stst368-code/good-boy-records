Global Metadata
Basic Attributes: Traditional British brass band street march in 6/8, with a brisk walking pace and two clear beats per bar.
Global Emotional Progression: Begin with the neat snap of a band rounding the corner and let it grow into a good-natured street singalong.
Application Scenarios & Imagery: A high-street parade passing close to brick shopfronts.
Sonics & Production Profile: Keep the mix open-air, with near-dry drums and short reflections from brick shopfronts. Let the quieter brass sit back when people sing.
Vocal Details
Vocal Gender & Timbre: Singer A is a male light tenor with clear British English diction, a bright chest tone and comfortable projection.
Vocal Style: Sing the longer lines plainly and breathe at the end of each pair. Keep the words distinct and let the group retain a little natural variation in timing.
Harmony/Backing Vocals: A small mixed group of men and women joins him in unison for the last two lines of the first refrain and the whole refrain on subsequent passes.
Vocal FX: Place the tenor close and the group a few feet back.
Arrangement
Instrument Lifecycle Description (Primary/Secondary Layering):
Primary: Use front-row cornets for the tune, tenor and baritone horns for the offbeats, euphoniums for moving replies, and trombones and tubas for weight.
Secondary: Side drum and bass drum hold the walking step, with a single cymbal accent on the return.
Groove & Foundation Progression: Two bars of side-drum taps cue a short unison cornet call. Tubas pick out root and fifth on the walking beats and tenor horns tick on the offbeats. As singing starts, the cornet row answers only in the gaps and bass drum stays light.

Open the first refrain with wider horn voicings and a lift from soprano cornet after each sung pair. A short cornet-and-euphonium exchange carries the march straight into the next sung passage. Keep the bass step unchanged and give the euphonium more room around the voice. On the next refrain add a single cymbal accent as the group comes in.

Thin to tenor horns and side drum for the quieter turn. Bring the whole band back with a two-bar pickup, keeping its loudest bursts between the sung lines. Let the last cornet call and the drum recede down the street together.
Embellishments, Textures & Spatial FX: Use the short brick reflections on cornet calls and let the last call recede with the drum.
