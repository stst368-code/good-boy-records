Global Metadata
Basic Attributes: roughly 210–240 BPM. key is minor. Black death metal.
Global Emotional Progression: Begin at maximum hostility and remain there. Verses, choruses and bridge should change through riff shape, vocal phrasing and cymbal pattern rather than through reduced intensity. Every section should feel like another stage of the same uncontrolled attack, with each new stage becoming more frantic and uncontrolled than the previous. The final section becomes even more frantic through denser blasting, harsher tremolo movement and increasingly desperate vocals before ending abruptly.
Application Scenarios & Imagery: An abandoned concrete environment, graffiti covered walls, late at night when its darkest, uncontrolled fires are the only source of light and heat as swarms of people dressed in dark clothing gather before the band on stage. The deafening amplifiers, sweat, feedback and a band performing with almost reckless physical intensity. Relentless, primitive, hostile and violent.
Sonics & Production Profile: Raw, abrasive and close. Guitars are sharp and grainy, bass is dirty but audible, drums are fast and physical, and the kick remains clearly perceptible beneath the blast beats. Avoid polished modern metal production, cinematic layers, orchestration, electronic elements and spacious atmospheric passages.
Vocal Details
Vocal Gender & Timbre: Singer A (Female), an extremely harsh female black-metal vocalist with piercing upper-register screams, torn rasps and occasional deeper throat-driven shrieks, the listener is unsure if she is singing or screaming during the more intense moments.
Vocal Style: From the start it is continuous extreme screaming with no clean singing, bracketed vocals sound like screaming whispering. Verses use frantic clipped phrases and savage sustained screams. Choruses become wider and more hysterical through doubled screams rather than melodic hooks. The bridge remains equally fast and violent, with prolonged desperate screams layered over uninterrupted blasting. The final passage should sound physically exhausted but increasingly ferocious.
Vocal FX: Minimal processing. Short dirty concrete room reverb, light saturation and occasional raw doubling. Keep the voice painfully close and human. During the most intense moments it may sound like the microphone is unable to keep up with the intensity of the singer.
Arrangement
Instrument Lifecycle Description (Primary/Secondary Layering):
Primary: Tremolo-picked distorted guitars, distorted bass and full-speed acoustic drums enter almost immediately and remain active throughout. Do not remove the drums, guitars or bass for transitions.
Secondary: Non instrumentation sound, which may be the lead singer smashing a bottle, or kicking a box to accent a pivotal moment in the song
Groove & Foundation Progression: Maintain continuous high-speed double-kick and blast-beat propulsion across intro, verses, choruses, bridge and outro. Never drop into half-time. Never introduce a breakdown. Never reduce the pulse for dramatic contrast. Section changes come from alternating blast-beat patterns, snare emphasis, ride or crash changes, tremolo riff substitutions and short fills while preserving maximum forward momentum.
Embellishments, Textures & Spatial FX: Brief amplifier feedback and cymbal wash may mark transitions. Avoid solos, avoid clean guitar, avoid acoustic passages, avoid ambient interludes and avoid extended instrumental pauses. Finish with either an instantaneous full-band cutoff or a final violent chord struck directly out of the ongoing blast.
