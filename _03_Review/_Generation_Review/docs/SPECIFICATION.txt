# GBR Review Tooling

## Purpose

- Local application for reviewing, rating, analysing and archiving generated GBR music.
- Designed for reviewing hundreds or thousands of generated tracks efficiently.
- Primary purpose:
  - Rapid human assessment of generated audio.
  - Consistent 0–5 quality ratings.
  - Centralised storage of reviewed FLAC/YAML pairs.
  - Automatic detection of potential audio defects.
  - Collection of structured data for aggregate analytics.
  - Identification of tracks desired for eventual publishing.
- Independent of the generation pipeline and publishing system.
- Human ratings are the primary measure of quality.
- Automated analysis supplements human reviews but does not influence or override ratings.

## 1. File Discovery and Loading

### Requirements

- Load `.flac` audio files.
- Configurable input directory or directories.
- Recursive searching of child folders.
- Support files scattered across multiple locations.
- Automatically discover newly generated files.
- Associate each FLAC with its corresponding generation YAML.
- Extract generation metadata from YAML.
- Where available, recover metadata embedded within FLAC files.
- Handle missing, invalid or incomplete YAMLs.
- Exclude already reviewed files from the normal review queue.
- Avoid duplicate indexing and repeated review of identical audio.
- Allow manual directory rescanning.

### Identification

- Generate SHA-256 hashes for FLAC files.
- Generate hashes for associated YAML files.
- Assign unique generation identifiers.
- Use audio hashes to identify identical recordings.
- Preserve separate generation records where identical audio has different generation metadata.
- Do not use filenames as primary identifiers.
- Retain original file paths and filenames in the database.

## 2. Audio Playback

### Full Playback

- Play complete FLAC recordings.
- Play, pause, resume, restart and seek.
- Volume control.
- Playback progress and duration.
- Clickable waveform for seeking.
- Optional playback-speed adjustment.
- Keyboard shortcuts on desktop.
- Large touch-friendly playback controls on mobile.
- Continue playback when navigating supported interface elements.

### Automatic Sampling

- Automatically select short, informative segments from each song.
- Provide a rapid overview without requiring full-track listening.
- Algorithmically identify:
  - Opening passage.
  - High-energy sections.
  - Low-energy sections.
  - Significant transitions.
  - Unusual or potentially defective sections.
  - Ending passage.
- Use audio analysis rather than fixed time intervals alone.
- Avoid selecting multiple overlapping or nearly identical segments.
- Preserve sufficient context around each selected section.
- Apply short fades where needed to avoid artificial clicks.
- Play snippets sequentially as one listening session.
- Allow the reviewer to replay individual snippets.
- Allow switching between automatic sampling and full playback.

### Sampling Modes

- Quick:
  - Approximately 15–25 seconds.
  - Focus on representative passages and potential anomalies.
- Standard:
  - Approximately 40–65 seconds.
  - Broader coverage of musical structure and consistency.
- Full:
  - Complete recording.
  - Manual seeking and section navigation.

Sampling durations configurable.

## 3. Human Rating System

### Four Rating Dimensions

Each dimension receives an independent integer score between 0 and 5.

**Audio Quality**
- Overall technical audio quality.
- Clarity and intelligibility.
- Unwanted noise, static or distortion.
- Digital artefacts.
- Clipping and other generation defects.
- Intentional musical distortion should not be penalised.

**Structure / Composition**
- Does the recording flow like a coherent song?
- Does it follow the intended genre?
- Does the arrangement make musical sense?
- Are the sections appropriately structured?
- Does the song develop and progress naturally?
- Does it sound complete?

**Consistency**
- Does the recording maintain its quality throughout?
- Does musical identity remain coherent?
- Are there sections that deteriorate into noise?
- Are there sudden unexplained changes in performance?
- Does the generation remain stable from beginning to end?
- Particular interest in failures associated with sampler/scheduler behaviour.

**Realism**
- Would an average listener believe the recording was produced by humans?
- Applies to all genres, including electronic, synthetic and chiptune music.
- Does not require natural instruments or human-sounding vocals.
- Evaluates the credibility of the overall production.
- Penalises recognisable AI-generation artefacts and unnatural execution.

### Rating Behaviour

- Six selectable values: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
- One selection required for each dimension.
- No mandatory written notes.
- No separate submit button.
- Automatically submit when all four ratings have been entered.
- Automatically advance to the next review.
- Back button to revisit and correct the previous rating.
- Skip button to move past a track without rating or archiving it.
- Skipped tracks remain available for later review.
- Preserve review corrections and history.
- Allow repeated reviews where explicitly requested.
- Record whether the assessment used snippets or complete playback.

### Score Calculation

- Four scores have equal weighting.
- Arithmetic mean calculated automatically.
- Preserve all four original scores.
- Preserve the unrounded average.
- Round the average to the nearest whole number.
- Use conventional half-up rounding:
  - 0.00–0.49 → 0
  - 0.50–1.49 → 1
  - 1.50–2.49 → 2
  - 2.50–3.49 → 3
  - 3.50–4.49 → 4
  - 4.50–5.00 → 5
- Rounded result determines the archive directory.
- Do not display the calculated average or rounded result during initial reviewing.
- Individual scores remain the authoritative assessment data.

## 4. Review Interface

### Design

- Extremely simple interface.
- Desktop and mobile compatible.
- Optimised for rapid repeated reviewing.
- No unnecessary statistics or technical information.
- Minimal navigation.
- Large, clearly separated controls.
- Responsive design for different screen sizes.

### Displayed Information

- Song title.
- Intended genre.
- Audio playback controls.
- Waveform.
- Optional spectral visualisation.
- Automatic sampling controls.
- Four rating categories.
- Six rating buttons per category.
- Back button.
- Skip button.

### Rating Buttons

- Display values 0–5 horizontally.
- Wide buttons, occupying available screen width.
- Large touch targets for mobile.
- Minimum target height approximately 48–52 pixels.
- Clear indication of selected value.
- Avoid accidental neighbouring selections.
- No scrolling required between rating dimensions where screen size permits.

### Hidden Information

Do not display:
- Model names.
- Model weights or precision.
- Sampler.
- Scheduler.
- CFG.
- Seeds.
- Steps.
- Encoder settings.
- Generation hardware.
- Generation duration.
- Previous scores.
- Calculated average.
- Archive destination.
- Automated defect severity.
- Other information likely to bias a human review.

Song title and intended genre are mandatory because they provide necessary context for evaluating composition and genre accuracy.

No separate technical-inspection mode is required.

Generation parameters remain accessible through the database for subsequent aggregate analytics in GBR.

## 6. Automatic Audio Analysis

### Purpose

- Identify potential generation defects.
- Locate interesting listening segments.
- Support automatic sampling.
- Store objective technical measurements.
- Enable later correlation between generation parameters and defects.
- Never automatically change human review scores.

### Analysis Categories

**Clipping and Peak Detection**
- Sample peak.
- True peak.
- Clipped samples.
- Sudden extreme waveform spikes.
- Abrupt transient irregularities.

**Loudness and Energy**
- RMS.
- Integrated LUFS.
- Short-term loudness.
- Loudness range.
- Sudden energy changes.
- Unexpected volume collapse.

**Spectral Analysis**
- Spectral centroid.
- Spectral flatness.
- Spectral flux.
- Frequency distribution.
- Broadband noise changes.
- Unusual high-frequency activity.
- Abrupt changes in spectral characteristics.

**Silence and Dropouts**
- Unexpected silence.
- Sudden signal loss.
- Extremely quiet sections.
- Abrupt starts or endings.
- Incomplete or truncated recordings.

**Stereo Analysis**
- Left/right channel balance.
- Stereo correlation.
- Phase behaviour.
- Sudden stereo collapse.
- Unusual channel divergence.

**Structural Analysis**
- Significant transitions.
- Repeated sections.
- Changes in energy.
- Section similarity.
- Changes in musical texture.
- Potential structural discontinuities.

**Generation Consistency**
- Analyse overlapping time windows.
- Compare neighbouring sections.
- Detect unexpected changes in spectral behaviour.
- Identify sustained noise-like degradation.
- Identify sections that diverge significantly from the remainder of the track.
- Detect possible sampler/scheduler-related instability.
- Distinguish potential anomalies from normal musical variation where possible.

### Detection Output

Each detected event records:
- Event identifier.
- Audio identifier.
- Detection type.
- Start timestamp.
- End timestamp.
- Severity score.
- Confidence score.
- Supporting measurements.
- Detection algorithm/version.

Detection is probabilistic. Unusual audio is not automatically defective.

### Reviewer Communication

- Display neutral points-of-interest markers on the waveform.
- Markers may represent:
  - Energy peaks.
  - Quiet passages.
  - Structural changes.
  - Possible defects.
- Use a consistent visual treatment.
- Do not explicitly label passages as defective before review completion.
- Allow clicking a marker to play the associated segment.
- Automatically incorporate useful markers into snippet selection.
- Keep detailed analysis findings hidden during initial scoring.

### Analysis Storage

- Store analysis separately from human reviews.
- Link results using audio hashes and internal identifiers.
- Preserve detection versions.
- Allow reanalysis when algorithms improve.
- Retain historical analysis if needed.
- Make results queryable independently and alongside human scores.

## 8. Centralised Archive

### Purpose

- Move reviewed media out of scattered generation directories.
- Keep reviewed files in one central location.
- Organise files by assessed quality.
- Preserve FLAC/YAML associations.
- Keep the archive simple to browse manually.

### Archive Structure

```text
GBR_Reviewed/
├── 0/
├── 1/
├── 2/
├── 3/
├── 4/
└── 5/
```

### Classification

- Automatically calculate the rounded average when reviewing completes.
- Move FLAC/YAML pair into corresponding `0–5` directory.
- Files remain together in the same directory.
- No mandatory per-song or per-generation subdirectories.
- High-scoring generations can coexist in the same directory.
- Identical scores do not require a forced winner.

### Naming

- Retain readable song names.
- Append unique identifiers to prevent collisions.
- Use a short audio hash where possible.
- Extend identifiers if a collision occurs.
- Use matching identifiers for FLAC and YAML.
- Never overwrite unrelated files.

Example:

```text
5/
├── the-endless-game__a91f42b8.flac
├── the-endless-game__a91f42b8.yaml
├── the-endless-game__e27c6501.flac
└── the-endless-game__e27c6501.yaml
```

For identical audio with different generation provenance, retain separate generation records and preserve the associated YAML information without duplicating or losing metadata.

### File Movement

- Automatically initiate archiving after completed review.
- Record review in SQLite before starting file operations.
- Support movement between different drives.
- Verify copied files before deleting originals.
- Prevent accidental overwriting.
- Handle missing files.
- Handle missing or malformed YAMLs.
- Recover from interrupted moves.
- Maintain a record of original and destination paths.
- Retry failed operations.
- Reconcile database state against the filesystem after unexpected shutdown.
- Never discard a completed review because a move failed.

### Rating Corrections

- Allow correction of previously completed reviews.
- Recalculate archive bucket.
- Automatically relocate files if their bucket changes.
- Preserve original review history.
- Update database paths and classifications.

## 9. Duplicate Detection and Comparison

### Duplicate Identification

- Detect identical FLAC audio using SHA-256.
- Detect duplicate generation records.
- Distinguish identical audio from separate recordings of the same song.
- Avoid unnecessary repeat reviews.
- Preserve differing YAML provenance even for identical audio.

### Equal Scores

- Allow multiple generations to receive identical scores.
- Do not artificially demote a generation because another scored equally.
- Unique filenames prevent Windows filesystem collisions.
- Equal-scoring files may coexist in the same archive directory.

### Optional A/B Review

- Allow comparison of multiple generations of the same song.
- Blind comparison where possible.
- Match playback loudness for fair comparisons.
- Select preferred generation.
- Preserve original 0–5 ratings.
- Store relative preference separately.
- Particularly useful when choosing between multiple high-scoring generations.
- Never force A/B comparison as a prerequisite for archiving.

## 10. Publishing Flag

### Scope

- Publishing is a completely separate system.
- GBR Review does not:
  - Generate album artwork.
  - Process lyrics.
  - Generate lighting controls.
  - Format publishing assets.
  - Upload media.
  - Publish songs.
  - Execute publishing workflows.

### Purpose

- Identify reviewed tracks desired for eventual GBR publication.
- Keep publishing intent separate from numerical quality ratings.
- Allow selection of a preferred generation when multiple high-quality versions exist.

### Eligibility

- Primary publishing candidates are tracks archived in directory `5`.
- Provide a secondary selection pass for high-scoring generations.
- Allow listening and comparison before selection.
- Allow multiple tracks to be selected where appropriate.
- Flagging a track does not change its quality ratings or archive directory.

### Publishing Metadata

Store publishing state within the SQLite database rather than modifying the generation YAML.

Suggested states:
- `not_selected`
- `selected`
- `published`

Additional optional states:
- `passed`
- `withdrawn`

### Functionality

- Filter archive bucket `5`.
- Review candidates.
- Mark desired tracks as `selected`.
- Remove selection if desired.
- Mark selected tracks as `published` later.
- Preserve dates of selection and publication.
- Preserve status-change history.
- Record optional external publishing references.
- Make publishing state queryable by external systems.

### External Integration

- Separate publishing system reads selected tracks from the database or an exported manifest.
- Expose audio location, generation YAML location, identifiers and publishing state.
- Publishing system controls its own processing and workflow.
- Review application only maintains the selection/status information.
- No automatic publishing trigger is required.

## 16. Primary Workflow

1. User configures one or more generation input directories.
2. Application recursively discovers FLAC files.
3. Corresponding generation YAML is located and parsed.
4. Files are identified and hashed.
5. Metadata is indexed in SQLite.
6. Audio-analysis worker calculates technical metrics.
7. Waveforms, spectrograms and sample segments are cached.
8. Unreviewed tracks enter the review queue.
9. Reviewer selects random, alphabetical or date ordering.
10. Application displays song title, intended genre and simple playback interface.
11. Reviewer listens to full audio or algorithmically selected snippets.
12. Reviewer assigns four 0–5 scores.
13. Selecting the fourth score automatically completes the review.
14. Individual scores and the calculated average are stored.
15. Average is rounded to an archive bucket between 0 and 5.
16. FLAC/YAML pair is moved into the appropriate central archive directory.
17. Destination files are verified and database paths updated.
18. Application advances to the next track.
19. Reviewer may go Back to correct the previous assessment.
20. Tracks in archive bucket 5 become available for optional publishing selection.
21. Reviewer can flag selected generations as desired for publication.
22. Publishing status is retained in the database for consumption by a separate system.
23. Complete human rating and machine-analysis datasets remain available to GBR analytics.

## 17. Secondary Publishing Selection Workflow

1. User opens the publishing-selection view.
2. Application lists reviewed tracks from archive bucket 5.
3. Reviewer listens to candidate tracks.
4. Optional A/B comparison between generations of the same song.
5. Reviewer flags desired items as `selected`.
6. Selection is saved in SQLite.
7. No media movement or publication occurs.
8. External publishing system can retrieve selected items.
9. Publishing status can subsequently be changed to `published`.
10. Original ratings, metadata and archive locations remain unchanged.

## 19. Core Design Rules

- **Keep reviewing simple.**
- **0–5 human ratings are the primary measure of quality.**
- Always display song title and intended genre.
- Never expose generation parameters during initial reviewing.
- Do not display calculated averages during reviewing.
- Automatically submit when all four ratings are populated.
- Use large mobile-friendly rating buttons.
- Automatic defect detection assists listening but does not determine ratings.
- Keep human scores and machine measurements separate.
- Archive reviewed FLAC/YAML pairs centrally by rounded average.
- Never force different scores merely to resolve duplicate filenames.
- Use hashing and unique identifiers for provenance and filename safety.
- Preserve generation metadata, review history and analysis results.
- Keep detailed analytics outside the review interface.
- Publishing selection is a simple database flag/status.
- Publishing itself is outside the application's scope.
- Maintain reliable, recoverable file operations.
- Optimise the complete workflow for reviewing large numbers of tracks with minimal interaction.
